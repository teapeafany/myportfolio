BERLIN EMOTIONSCAPES

a data visualization (Fall 2025)

how do invisible cultural boundaries shape lived emotional experience?

I made this map trying to answer a question I couldn’t quite articulate: why did certain neighborhoods feel like mine, and others didn’t?

I overlaid my emotional journey across Berlin this summer with the path of the Wall, that scar the city has been growing over for thirty years. What I found surprised me. I was happier, consistently, in the East. The regions that spent decades under the GDR.

I sat with that for a while.

The differences are subtle if you’re not looking. Trams in the East, U-Bahns in the West. Vietnamese restaurants where Chinese ones aren’t. A certain quietness west of the Wall, a certain aliveness east of it. After reunification, artists flooded into the East because the rent was cheap, and they left something behind. That creative restlessness still lives in Friedrichshain, in Mitte, in pockets of Prenzlauer Berg. I think my body knew before my brain did.

Plotting the Wall’s remains unlocked other things too. The solemnness at the Memorial. A dead pigeon on the way out that I haven’t stopped thinking about. The exhaustion of Potsdamer Platz, too many buildings, too many cars, nowhere for your eyes to rest. The sudden lift when I turned into Mitte and the trams appeared.

The map didn’t give me answers. It gave me better questions. I’d want to come back with this data and sit with people who actually live here, to find out if they feel the division too, in ways they’ve never had words for either.
